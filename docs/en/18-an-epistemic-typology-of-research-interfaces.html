V. Research Artefacts and Comparative Cases

An Epistemic Typology of Research Interfaces

Chapter 18Draft

Typing by epistemic activity

Research interfaces are ordinarily described by their technology or by the data format they consume, which says little about what they are for. The typology proposed here classifies them by the primary epistemic activity they support, so that two artefacts built on the same stack fall into different types where they support different scholarly work, and no particular software form is constitutive of any of them.1 What justifies typing at this level is that the interpretative content of an artefact lies in its structures and interactions rather than in its implementation, which means a classification by technology sorts by the least informative property available.2

The documented practice supplies the recurring forms. Capture makes a data model actionable during structured data creation. Transformation maps source or research data into another explicit representation. Exploration supports investigation, comparison, filtering, and navigation. Edition makes an editorial account of texts and their relations operational through transformation, presentation, navigation, and annotation. Verification supports the inspection of outputs, evidence, checking procedures, and differentiated states. Integrated scholarly workbenches combine several of these for a bounded research task.3 The types are combinable by construction, so a single promptotype may realise several of them at once.3

The types

Exploration interfaces

Exploration interfaces enable investigation through filters, linked views, alternative navigation, comparison, and visualisation. Their epistemic function is to make a body of data traversable along more than one path, so that a question can be pursued in a form the data support. They carry the risk that comes with visual fluency, since a technically possible visualisation may imply a degree of precision, completeness, or certainty the data cannot warrant, which is a finding the documented cases produced repeatedly.4

Verification interfaces

Verification interfaces expose intermediate or final outputs beside sources, ground truth, or alternative procedures so that errors can be identified and corrected. They are the artefact form of the distinction between inspecting an output and authorising it, since they make differentiated states visible and thereby let the capacity to inspect be exercised without conferring the authority to record a result as verified.5

Edition interfaces

Edition interfaces connect encoded texts with facsimiles, apparatus, translations, annotations, and editorial interventions. The documented proposal-stage case shows the form at its most economical, in that a bounded path led from a limited textual sample through an encoded representation to a statically hosted edition interface, which made a proposed editorial arrangement examinable before a production corpus existed.6

Capture interfaces

Capture interfaces support structured data entry, annotation, correction, and metadata creation, and their second function is diagnostic. Where a data model is still developing, an interface lets project partners enter, display, filter, and compare information in a shared operational environment rather than discussing the model through schemas alone, which tests the adequacy of the model against the records it has to hold.7

Audit interfaces

Audit interfaces expose processing histories, disagreements, provenance, evaluation results, transformations, and decision pathways.8 Their subject is the research process rather than the research material. What the documented practice supports is the underlying requirement, since accountability in agent-supported work rests on a reconstructable relation among sources, maintained project knowledge, versioned implementation, differentiated evidence, and responsible judgement, and that relation has to be made inspectable somewhere.9

What the typology is for

The types are not mutually exclusive, and the point of the exercise is to name the epistemic role an artefact plays rather than to sort artefacts into boxes. A single artefact may combine several functions, as the documented workbenches do, and the analytical dimensions along which promptotypes are compared may overlap and change between iterations, so that a data-capture interface also supports exploration and a production workflow later becomes a handover package or an object of methodological examination.10

Read against the methodological functions of implementation, the typology answers a different question. The functions of production and curation, prospective modelling, and project formation describe what implementation does for the project, while the interface types describe what the resulting artefact lets a reader or user do.11 A capture interface built during prospective modelling and a capture interface built inside a production workflow are the same type and serve different functions, which is why both classifications are needed.

A typology of this kind is a working instrument and not a claim about the space of possible artefacts. The documented corpus supports the workability and range of the method within the practice it was consolidated from, and it establishes no complete typology of promptotypes.12 The five types collected here are the ones the documented artefacts occupy, and a further type would be added when an artefact appears whose epistemic activity none of them names.

Gaps

  • The five interface types come from the outline, while the sources name six operational forms of a promptotype. Exploration, verification, edition, and capture appear in both; transformation and the integrated workbench appear only in the sources; the audit interface appears only in the outline. The chapter carries the outline's five, notes the difference, and grounds the audit type on a posit and on the accountability requirement.
  • The paper's grouping of cases around evaluation and audit, which would give the audit type its documented instances, is a section of the paper this chapter does not draw on directly. The case treatment belongs to chapter 19, where those instances are named.
  • Whether the typology is the book's own construction or rests on a source is recorded as an open question in the topic map and is not settled here.
