# Distillate: Full Lecture Notes, German

The German Full Lecture Notes carry the numbered chapter structure of the teaching line and supply this vault with the definitional core of the four engineering layers, the digital-edition example that runs through all of them, and the verification vocabulary that separates technical conformance from scholarly acceptance.

## Core statements

- Productive and traceable work with LLM-based AI agents requires organised project knowledge, task-specific context, controlled agentic execution and responsible checking together, and follows from neither better models nor better prompt wording alone. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0100]] ^s1
- Four terms structure the field, Prompt Engineering for the current input sequence, Knowledge Engineering for the maintained body of knowledge, Context Engineering for the information state of a task, and Agentic Engineering for multi-step execution. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0101]] ^s2
- Prompt Engineering shapes the current input sequence. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0102]] ^s3
- Knowledge Engineering builds and maintains the available body of knowledge. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0103]] ^s4
- Context Engineering assembles the information state required for a concrete task. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0104]] ^s5
- Agentic Engineering organises multi-step execution inside a technical environment. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0105]] ^s6
- The AI harness mediates between the four layers by providing tools and access, managing the state of a piece of work and returning results to the model, and it does not decide which editorial reading is adequate or whether an artefact may be accepted for publication. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0106]] ^s7
- An LLM-based AI agent can inspect project resources, combine information from different sources, generate and execute code, process error messages and adapt its further procedure to the observed project state. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0083]] ^s8
- The ability to produce an artefact is not the ability to judge its technical correctness or its scholarly adequacy reliably. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0085]] ^s9
- The central challenge lies in organising the conditions under which results are produced, checked, revised and used for a named purpose. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0086]] ^s10
- A single precisely worded instruction can suffice for a simple and clearly bounded task, and stops being sufficient once a task spans several files, tools and decisions. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0087]] ^s11
- The questions raised by a multi-file editorial task belong to different layers, one part to the design of the current task, one to persistent project knowledge, one to the selection of task-specific context and one to the control of multi-step execution. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0096]] ^s12
- Source images, transcription, data model, transformation and frontend display are not independent of one another, and a modelling rule can turn out to be inadequate only in the frontend display. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0114]] ^s13
- A large language model produces text by computing probabilities for the next token from the input sequence so far, and the selected token becomes part of the context for the next prediction. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0126]] ^s14
- The output of a large language model remains probabilistic, so the same prompt can produce different results across several runs, and a plausible formulation is therefore not automatically a reconstructed fact. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0127]] ^s15
- A model can produce a convincing transcription with individual characters read wrongly and a formally plausible TEI structure that does not follow the project guidelines, so the linguistic quality of an answer must not be confused with its scholarly reliability. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0128]] ^s16
- Pre-training builds statistical representations of linguistic and subject-matter patterns that are not addressable like entries in a database, and the model holds no reliable register of pages or sources. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0129]] ^s17
- Post-training through instruction tuning, reinforcement learning from human feedback and related procedures turns a base model into a system that answers as a helpful assistant and observes safety and style rules. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0130]] ^s18
- The assistant figure appearing in an interaction is not equivalent to a human counterpart. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0131]] ^s19
- The assistant figure is a behavioural pattern stabilised by training, system instructions and current context, which explains why matter-of-fact structured communication is often productive and why social coherence must not be confused with human intentionality or subject-matter authority. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0134]] ^s20
- An isolated model call produces an output, while an agent pursues a goal across several model and tool calls, inspects its environment, selects an action, observes the result and updates its procedure. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0135]] ^s21
- The LLM forms the flexible planning and interpretation module of an agent, and its actual scope of action arises only through tools and a working environment. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0146]] ^s22
- An AI harness is the technical software layer through which an LLM-based agent receives context, calls tools, accesses files, executes programs and processes feedback. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0147]] ^s23
- A harness can fix which folders may be read or changed, which commands run without confirmation, how tool outputs return into the model context, how long a run continues, when a human must be involved and how intermediate results are stored. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0148]] ^s24
- The harness supplies technical means of control and does not decide whether an editorial modelling decision is scholarly adequate, just as an XML validator can establish that an element is admissible at a position without deciding whether the source is actually illegible. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0155]] ^s25
- A prompt is the input sequence supplied for a model call and can contain a task, source material, context information, requirements, constraints, examples, procedural notes and specifications for the expected output. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0156]] ^s26
- Prompt Engineering denotes the iterative development of individual prompts by changing their content, their structure or the prompting techniques applied to them. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0157]] ^s27
- The term Prompt Engineering is deliberately narrower than Context Engineering and addresses a concrete input sequence rather than the whole information environment of a longer working trajectory. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0160]] ^s28
- A more bounded and checkable version of a transcription instruction makes visible which properties of the result are expected, and it reduces the number of silent assumptions without guaranteeing a correct transcription. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0165]] ^s29
- A good prompt is a bounded specification that describes a current task inside an already existing context of knowledge and work. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0166]] ^s30
- The typical components of a bounded specification are goal, initial situation, requirements, constraints, procedure, output form and completion criterion. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0167]] ^s31
- The precision of a prompt does not depend on its length, since a very long prompt can be contradictory or hard to prioritise while a short prompt can suffice when the relevant rules already exist in knowledge documents and instruction files. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0176]] ^s32
- A task prompt that refers to the persistent body of knowledge instead of restating the editorial rules stays compact and bounded to the current task. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0192]] ^s33
- Assigning a role can influence terminology, style, perspective and level of detail, and it adds no new subject knowledge to the model, at most making learned patterns of specialist communication more probable. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0195]] ^s34
- Role prompting denotes a brief functional assignment and persona prompting an elaborated perspective with background, experience, goals, constraints and situation of use, and the two should be considered together without being equated. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0196]] ^s35
- A synthetic persona can make possible problems visible and produces no empirical user data, so its answers are hypotheses that must be checked against real persons, observations or existing user research. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0212]] ^s36
- Role and persona prompting suit style variation, changes of perspective, early interface and material critique, the preparation of interviews and the identification of possible queries. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0213]] ^s37
- Role and persona prompting replace neither subject knowledge nor real stakeholders, empirical user research or scholarly validation. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0219]] ^s38
- A productive interaction can consist of several bounded passes in the order generate, check, correct, condense. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0225]] ^s39
- Self-revision can make errors visible and is no independent verification, because the same model can overlook its own false assumptions or justify them plausibly after the fact, and it becomes more reliable with explicit criteria, external tests and checkable references. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0239]] ^s40
- Structured output formats reduce ambiguity, and the levels of syntactic conformance, structural completeness, semantic correctness and scholarly adequacy have to be distinguished. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0240]] ^s41
- Valid JSON proves only that the syntax is right and schema-valid TEI only that the formal rules were observed, while whether the source is adequately represented remains a question of subject expertise. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0245]] ^s42
- Studies report effects of emotional additions, of politeness and of unusual automatically generated prompts, while other investigations show that irrelevant additions degrade performance or that effects do not replicate stably on newer models. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0249]] ^s43
- Prompt effects are frequently local and depend on model and model version, task and dataset, language, position and structure of the information, evaluation metric and random variation. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0250]] ^s44
- A changed performance after a prompt variation does not prove that the wording works for the assumed reason, and such explanations remain hypotheses as long as no direct mechanistic evidence exists. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0257]] ^s45
- Prompt variants should be treated as experimental interventions. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0258]] ^s46
- The procedure for testing a prompt variant fixes goal and metric, defines a baseline, changes only one relevant component, uses several examples and repetitions, checks on new cases, documents model and version, and assesses subject quality separately from style and format. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0262]] ^s47
- Linguistic inputs are transferred into high-dimensional numerical representations, and different formulations change the internal activations from which the probability distribution over possible outputs arises. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0269]] ^s48
- The expression Latent Program Space can be read as a metaphor for the set of learned processing routines that a model activates differently depending on input and context, and translating, summarising, classifying or explaining are recurring behavioural patterns rather than clearly separated programs. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0273]] ^s49
- Attribution graphs and related procedures show that particular internal structures and activation directions can be connected with observable behaviour, and they supply no complete theory with which natural prompt effects could be predicted reliably. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0277]] ^s50
- Prompt Engineering can make a current task more precise and cannot on its own solve fundamental problems of knowledge and work organisation. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0278]] ^s51
- A good prompt replaces neither missing or inaccessible project knowledge, contradictory guidelines, unresolved requirements, an overloaded or irrelevant context, persistent documentation, tool and permission management, technical tests, scholarly validation nor the organisation of longer working trajectories. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0279]] ^s52
- The longer and more resource-rich a task becomes, the less it can be described as the optimisation of a single input sequence, and the object shifts from the wording of the prompt to the organisation of the information state in which the agent works. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0289]] ^s53
- The complete body of a project is relevant to the project while not all of it has to lie in the model context at the same time. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0320]] ^s54
- Further resources can remain in the project holdings and be read or queried through tools when needed. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0327]] ^s55
- Context Engineering denotes the systematic selection, organisation, maintenance and provision of the task-specific information state, and it determines the form and order of the information, when further information is loaded and what deliberately stays outside the current context. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0328]] ^s56
- A model processes only the information actually supplied within a current run, which can include system and project instructions, the current user input, the working history so far, document excerpts, tool descriptions, tool outputs, intermediate results and the generated answer. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0329]] ^s57
- The technically bounded processing space is called the context window, and its nominal size states how many tokens a system can process without stating that all contained information is used equally reliably. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0338]] ^s58
- Investigations of long contexts show that position, distraction and volume of the supplied information can influence performance, and that relevant information can be harder to find in long inputs when it stands between similar or contradictory content. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0339]] ^s59
- Context Rot denotes observed drops in performance as context length grows, it is no single conclusively clarified technical mechanism, and it remains useful for agentic work because tool outputs, failed attempts, obsolete plans and intermediate states accumulate during longer runs and compete with currently relevant information. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0340]] ^s60
- Too strong a reduction can remove conditions, uncertainties and provenance, and the target is a dense and sufficient context, as bounded as possible and as complete and differentiated as the task requires. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0341]] ^s61
- A file in the project folder is not automatically part of the context window, and the system must read, extract, transform or inspect it through a tool for its content to become available to the model. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0342]] ^s62
- The path of information runs from file or data holding through tool, parser or script to the supplied representation, then through tokenisation into the context window. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0344]] ^s63
- Different formats require different forms of access, so Markdown and source code can usually be read directly while databases are used through queries without being loaded into the context in full. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0345]] ^s64
- A script can count only those elements of a hundred TEI files that are relevant to a current modelling question, and what enters the model context is then the output of the script rather than the entire data holding. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0352]] ^s65
- The methodologically relevant unit is the representation supplied to the model rather than the file as such. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0353]] ^s66
- Context Compression reduces the amount of directly supplied context through selection of relevant sections, summary, removal of repetition, aggregation of data, choice of representative examples and compaction of a working history. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0355]] ^s67
- A shorter version is not automatically a better one, because a summary can smooth uncertainty, remove justifications or turn several alternative statements into an apparently unambiguous rule. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0362]] ^s68
- Distillation goes beyond mere compression and transfers available understanding into a selective, structured, inspectable and revisable representation. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0363]] ^s69
- Distillation comprises the three operations of selection, structuring and condensation. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0370]] ^s70
- The counter-risk is over-distillation, which occurs when provenance, uncertainties or details necessary for action are lost. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0374]] ^s71
- The Project Knowledge Base preserves the persistent, inspectable and revisable body of knowledge. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0378]] ^s72
- The Working Context is the information state assembled for a concrete task. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0379]] ^s73
- The context window is the technical processing space in which that context is used by the model. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0380]] ^s74
- General project reports, guidelines for other source types, complete logs, old schema versions and failed attempts that are no longer relevant need not be contained in the working context of a single encoding task. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0391]] ^s75
- Knowledge Engineering builds and maintains the body of knowledge, and Context Engineering assembles from it and from further resources the context required for a concrete task. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0398]] ^s76
- Knowledge in a project is often distributed across guidelines, correspondence, encoded examples and the experience of individuals, and long-standing participants supply missing connections unconsciously while a new person or a new agent instance lacks that background. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0409]] ^s77
- An agent can search all files without a consistent project understanding arising from that, and it can mix an old rule with a new one or derive a general convention from a single example. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0410]] ^s78
- Knowledge Engineering concerns the construction and maintenance of explicit, inspectable and revisable project knowledge and makes relevant terms, rules, decisions, constraints and uncertainties visible enough to be read, criticised and continued. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0411]] ^s79
- A knowledge base is purpose-bound and holds the part of the knowledge required for particular forms of work, decision and checking rather than a complete representation of all available information. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0412]] ^s80
- Project knowledge comes from two basic sources, existing documents and data, and the implicit knowledge of persons and organisations. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0413]] ^s81
- Implicit knowledge covers reasons for earlier decisions, known exceptions, expectations of the target artefact, criteria for acceptance and practical experience with particular source types. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0424]] ^s82
- Knowledge Acquisition denotes the elicitation and explication of relevant knowledge through document analysis, interviews, workshops, observation of workflows, error analysis and joint modelling sessions. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0430]] ^s83
- An interview can surface a rule that has been practised for years without standing in the old guideline, Knowledge Acquisition makes it visible, and distillation transfers it into a checkable form. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0431]] ^s84
- A collection of files is not yet a knowledge base. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0432]] ^s85
- A Project Knowledge Base is the persistent, inspectable and revisable holding of documented project knowledge and records the current understanding the project has of its data, its purpose and the relevant decisions. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0433]] ^s86
- The knowledge base does not replace sources and research data, it describes and contextualises their use, and a source description is not the source just as a data-model document is not the encoded file. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0447]] ^s87
- A holding distributed across a long guideline, several messages and orally transmitted experience is hard to use for a concrete task, because an agent would have to search the relevant statements anew each time and could mix contradictory versions. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0448]] ^s88
- A knowledge document brings the statements relevant to a bounded subject together in a checkable form. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0449]] ^s89
- A knowledge document is a bounded, structured and revisable representation of relevant knowledge that can be distilled from a more extensive holding, checked by humans and used as context by LLM-based systems. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0450]] ^s90
- The important properties of a knowledge document are a clearly bounded subject, a traceable structure, visible uncertainties, documented provenance, revisability and dual readability for humans and LLM-based systems. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0451]] ^s91
- The knowledge document is a conceptual artefact and is not bound to a particular file format, and Markdown is used in this workflow because it is open, text-based and readable both for humans and for LLM-based systems. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0489]] ^s92
- Markdown is readable with different editors, versionable line by line, easy to reference, linkable in Obsidian or comparable systems and can be taken selectively into working contexts. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0496]] ^s93
- Markdown does not make content correct and only creates a form in which humans and agents can work on the same documented holding. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0502]] ^s94
- Not every piece of persistent information is a knowledge document. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0503]] ^s95
- A knowledge document describes what is known about a subject. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0504]] ^s96
- An instruction file fixes recurring rules for agentic work, for example in a repository-level instruction document. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0508]] ^s97
- An agent skill bundles instructions, scripts and resources for a recurring class of tasks. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0516]] ^s98
- A knowledge document describes the subject, an instruction file governs the recurring work, a skill operationalises a reusable procedure, and a prompt formulates the current task. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0522]] ^s99
- Knowledge bases lose usability without maintenance, documents become outdated, terms inconsistent and parallel versions contradictory. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0523]] ^s100
- Governance determines the rules for construction, change and use, and curation applies those rules to the concrete holding. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0524]] ^s101
- Structural curation concerns file names, metadata, links, document types, version information and duplicates. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0525]] ^s102
- Substantive curation concerns contradictory statements, outdated rules, missing constraints, inappropriate condensations and the revision of requirements. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0532]] ^s103
- An agent can localise problems and generate proposals, and changes with substantive consequences must be checked and answered for. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0538]] ^s104
- With growing task duration the number of places at which errors can enter later steps rises alongside the possible performance of an agent. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0555]] ^s105
- An agent that reads an outdated guideline can generate an unsuitable encoding pattern, transform it and adapt the frontend to the wrong structure, so that every single step looks technically plausible while the original error is carried through the whole working trajectory. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0556]] ^s106
- Agentic Engineering denotes the systematic organisation and control of multi-step agentic work and concerns the bounding and decomposition of tasks, tool use, the processing of intermediate results, states and handovers, conditions for abort and escalation, and checking and continuation. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0557]] ^s107
- The central question is under which conditions the actions of an agent remain traceable, bounded and correctable rather than only whether an agent can act. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0564]] ^s108
- A simplified execution loop runs from capturing the state through planning the next step and executing a tool or action to observing the result and updating the procedure. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0568]] ^s109
- Autonomy denotes the extent of work between two human interventions and does not mean the absence of human control. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0580]] ^s110
- A plan should determine which sub-problems exist, which information is missing, which tools are needed, which checks are foreseen and which order makes sense. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0581]] ^s111
- An extensive plan drawn up before the project holdings have been inspected can create false certainty, and good plans are compact, checkable against the current state and revisable. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0587]] ^s112
- Feedback can come from validators, tests, error messages, tool outputs, reviews by other agents, human responses and changed requirements. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0588]] ^s113
- Tools extend an LLM from a text generator into a system that can act on an environment, and they include file access, terminal, code execution, web search, database queries, browser control, validators and specialised interfaces. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0597]] ^s114
- A tool call can change the project state, so access should be granted according to the principle of the least necessary permission. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0606]] ^s115
- Source files may be read and not overwritten, generated files may be changed in a working folder, validators may run without confirmation, publication or deployment steps need an explicit release, and changes should remain versioned and reversible. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0611]] ^s116
- The Model Context Protocol standardises the connection of LLM applications with tools and data sources, and a server of this protocol can provide access to a repository, a database or a validator. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0613]] ^s117
- The protocol solves a technical integration problem and does not decide whether a tool suits the task or how its results are to be interpreted in subject terms. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0614]] ^s118
- A subagent is a bounded agent instance with a partial task. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0615]] ^s119
- More agents do not automatically produce better results, they raise coordination and checking effort, and every subagent needs a clear assignment, bounded context, a defined return format and rules for uncertainty. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0620]] ^s120
- Agent-to-agent protocols connect independent agents while the methodological questions of responsibility, transferred information, visibility of conflicts and decision in case of contradictory results remain. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0621]] ^s121
- A useful intermediate state is executable or inspectable, assignable to a defined project state, checkable against requirements and small enough to reconstruct the causes of an error. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0626]] ^s122
- Intermediate results should not remain exclusively in the chat history, and relevant plans, decisions, check results and open questions belong in persistent project artefacts. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0631]] ^s123
- Typical points of human intervention are contradictory requirements, missing scholarly foundations, changes that are hard to reverse, sensitive resources, modelling decisions with subject-matter consequences, and validation and acceptance. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0632]] ^s124
- Agents can collect evidence and do not thereby acquire the authority to validate a result in subject terms or to accept it for a purpose. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0639]] ^s125
- The reflection questions of the frontend exercise ask whether uncertainties are displayed as uncertainties, whether the interface produces false unambiguity, which modelling problems become visible only in the interface and which knowledge documents must be revised. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0657]] ^s126
- Promptotyping is an iterative, document-based method for developing project-specific digital research artefacts with LLM-based AI agents. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0663]] ^s127
- Promptotyping does not denote the mere production of a prototype through prompts and places the joint development of documented project understanding, digital research artefact, checking procedures and bounded reasons for acceptance at its centre. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0664]] ^s128
- The basic cycle runs from project knowledge through working context and agentic implementation to the digital research artefact, then through checking to the revision of project knowledge. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0672]] ^s129
- The artefact and the documented project understanding develop further together. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0673]] ^s130
- Preparation makes data, sources, standards and research context accessible and creates a traceable initial state beyond file management. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0682]] ^s131
- Exploration investigates what the data and sources make possible and where they set limits. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0683]] ^s132
- Exploration produces no final specification and makes visible which questions must be clarified and which decisions documented. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0690]] ^s133
- Distillation transfers the developed understanding into maintained documents that remain revisable because new findings from implementation can change earlier assumptions. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0698]] ^s134
- Scholar-Centred Design aligns development with the research practices, interpretative tasks and responsibilities of the participating researchers and asks which scholarly distinctions a surface makes visible or invisible. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0707]] ^s135
- Implementation is not the neutral execution of a fully determined specification, because a missing rule or a modelling that is too coarse can become visible only through the working artefact. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0715]] ^s136
- A finding that a frontend forces a finer distinction than the data model provides belongs not only in the code but back in the knowledge document and where necessary in the data model. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0716]] ^s137
- Technical verification checks conformance with formalised requirements such as well-formedness, schema conformance, passing tests, expected transformation outputs and unchanged source files. [[10_markdown/documents/lecture-notes-de#^p0720]] ^s138
- Scholarly validation checks whether representation and artefact are adequate to the intended research purpose, covering fidelity to the source, adequate representation of editorial uncertainty, support for the intended interpretative actions and whether modelling decisions are made visible or misleadingly naturalised. ## Open questions

- The lecture notes describe the four engineering layers for a teaching setting and do not state how the layers are bounded against one another when a project runs several of them at once.
- The notes hold no definition of implementation drift or of the accumulation of unchecked generated output, although both follow from the error-propagation passage of chapter 6.
- The notes name a principle of least necessary permission without a procedure for deciding which permission an individual task needs.
- The relation between the German chapter numbering and the prose headings of the English lecture notes is not stated in either source and was reconstructed by this vault from the parallel content.
- The notes state that self-revision becomes more reliable with explicit criteria and external tests without saying how much of the residual error such measures remove.

## Appraisal

The German lecture notes are the author's own teaching text and stand at the state of a working version. They carry the sharpest definitional formulations of the four layers available in the feeding set, because a teaching text has to fix a term before it can be used, and their digital-edition example holds the argument together across chapters in a way the slide deck cannot. Their limit is the genre. Claims about model behaviour rest on short citations of the research literature rather than on their own evidence, the empirical passages on prompt effects report a state of research without weighing it, and the hands-on sections are exercise material rather than findings. Where this vault needs an empirical claim about model behaviour, the underlying publications will have to enter as sources of their own. The notes are treated here as the authoritative statement of the vocabulary and as a secondary report of the research literature.
