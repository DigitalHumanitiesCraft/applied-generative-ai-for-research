# Distillate: Vault als materialisiertes Wissensmodell

The vault document is the theoretical text of the author's own knowledge practice and supplies this vault with what neither the lecture notes nor the slide deck carry, the reading of a knowledge holding as a model whose structure is itself meaning-bearing, the properties and the test of a single knowledge document, the five transformations that produce such documents, and the older disciplinary traditions in which those transformations stand.

## Core statements

- A vault of plain-text notes is a materialised semantic net whose structure of links, tags and hierarchies itself carries meaning and can be fed selectively into the contexts of language models. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0001]] ^v1
- Understanding the vault as a knowledge model makes every vault operation a modelling operation that actively forms a model of one's own knowledge in the act of filing information. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0001]] ^v2
- The unit of this model is the knowledge document as an atomic distilled knowledge structure readable by humans and usable as context by language models, and its dynamics are the knowledge transformations, systematic operations that convert raw material into such units. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0002]] ^v3
- Unit and dynamics together make the vault an external memory whose output quality depends on the quality of its knowledge model. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0002]] ^v4
- A link that is set, a tag that is chosen and a division of a document into smaller units determine how the contained knowledge can later be interpreted, by a human reader as much as by a language model. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0004]] ^v5
- Every structural decision is thereby an epistemic one, because it fixes which connections are visible, which concepts count as independent and how the net behaves when it is loaded selectively into a context. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0004]] ^v6
- Three implications follow for practice, that structural work on the holding is modelling work rather than filing, that the holding becomes an external memory which can be loaded into contexts with the quality of the model bounding the quality of the output, and that new knowledge arises above all through the transformations. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0005]] ^v7
- The calibration between the two directions of reading stays open, because a knowledge model for the context of a language model and one for human reading can demand different granularity, and the possible conflict between compact machine readability and detailed human traceability is not conclusively resolved. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0006]] ^v8
- How the quality of a knowledge model could be measured at all is equally open. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0006]] ^v9
- A knowledge document is an atomic, structured knowledge unit produced by distillation from raw material such as sources, experiences and data, and optimised as a context artefact for the collaboration between humans and language models. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0007]] ^v10
- In Context Engineering the knowledge document functions as materialised context compression, a condensation performed in advance that a model no longer has to reconstruct from the raw material itself. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0007]] ^v11
- The format rests on three properties at once, dual readability for humans and as context for language models, compactness with maximal information at minimal token cost, and portability as a plain-text file that can be used, linked and exchanged in knowledge systems. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0008]] ^v12
- A good knowledge document is transferable, compact and retrievable, so that the knowledge stays applicable to situations unknown at the time of writing, only what the application needs is present, and clear headings, metadata and a logical structure make the knowledge quickly reactivatable. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0009]] ^v13
- Such a document is neither textbook nor tutorial, it presupposes that the reader knows the context or can work it out, and it delivers the concentrated principle. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0009]] ^v14
- The distillation process follows a staged logic from a concrete case through the extraction of patterns and their abstraction into principles detached from the single case to a condensation into a storable and retrievable form. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0010]] ^v15
- The result is applicable independently of the originating example, so a reader of the document can transfer the principles to other contexts without ever having seen the original material. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0010]] ^v16
- A knowledge document thereby stores thinking tools rather than facts, and the test of a successful one is whether someone who reads only the document can apply the principle to a new case without asking questions. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0010]] ^v17
- Knowledge transformations are systematic operations that convert information into reusable, contextualised knowledge structures, and five operations carry the procedure. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0011]] ^v18
- The five operations are compression or distillation, normalisation, enrichment, consolidation and atomisation, each defined by its input, its output and the direction of its information flow. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0012]] ^v19
- Atomisation divides a monolithic record in which many separately referenceable concepts lie mixed into several atomic documents, one concept each and separately findable and linkable, while the original document becomes a map that points to them. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0013]] ^v20
- Consolidation merges several documents with redundant foundations and a fragmented presentation into one comprehensive document with a continuous line in which the redundancy is removed. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0013]] ^v21
- The two operations work in opposite directions, one dividing and one merging, and both serve the same goal of the clean assignment of one concept to one unit. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0013]] ^v22
- Four design principles guide the transformations, the atomic principle that one concept inhabits exactly one document, self-containedness, information density with maximal information per token and without redundancy across documents, and provenance secured through a sources section and frontmatter recording creation, source and status. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0014]] ^v23
- Self-containedness requires that every document stays understandable on its own with the necessary context inside the document rather than only in its links, which is decisive for its use as context for a language model. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0014]] ^v24
- The quality criteria for use as context concern length, structure, context and redundancy, so a good document is of middling size with frontmatter, sections and sources, self-explanatory and free of duplicated content across files. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0016]] ^v25
- The workflow with model assistance runs from the identified input through the choice of the fitting transformation and its assisted execution in the format of the holding to integration through links and entries in the relevant maps and a closing validation of self-containedness and substance. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0017]] ^v26
- The five operations are each anchored in traditions of information and documentation science and of knowledge management, and the document records a web-supported literature check of those anchors with its date. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0018]] ^v27
- Compression or distillation corresponds to the abstracting of documentation science, codified as theory and technique in the mid-1970s and normed as a procedure in an international standard, so condensation is a standardised procedure with a rule set. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0019]] ^v28
- Normalisation corresponds to formal description according to a rule set, integrated conceptually under the notion of bibliographic languages and carried terminologically in the German-language library tradition. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0020]] ^v29
- Enrichment corresponds to subject indexing and semantic description as the descriptive and contextualising side of information organisation. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0021]] ^v30
- Consolidation corresponds to information consolidation, which separates the restructuring of content from the repackaging of form, and to the combination mode of a knowledge creation model in which explicit knowledge is newly brought together. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0022]] ^v31
- Atomisation corresponds to the monographic principle of documentation, which breaks knowledge into smallest units detached from the document unit and recombinable and reclassifiable, and to a card-index practice with atomic, fixed-address, cross-linked note units. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0023]] ^v32
- Elicitation as the second source of knowledge corresponds to the externalisation mode of the knowledge creation model, which is a cycle at organisational level, so the assignment to document operations is a transfer rather than a direct correspondence. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0024]] ^v33
- The provenance principle has its machine-readable form in the W3C provenance recommendation with entity, activity, agent and a derivation relation, and its research-practical demand in the FAIR principles. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0024]] ^v34
- The whole procedure of putting knowledge into filable and retrievable documents is the knowledge codification of knowledge management, with a stage model of capturing, packaging and reusing. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0024]] ^v35
- No canonical taxonomy of document transformations exists in either discipline, and an established classification of knowledge processes into creation, storage and retrieval, transfer and application lies across the document operations, so the five-part systematics is the author's own coinage. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0025]] ^v36
- The five-part systematics can be derived as a facetted classification over two axes, at the unit boundaries by dividing or merging and at the unit itself by reducing content, extending content or reordering form at constant content, and its completeness holds given that definition of the axes while composite procedures are compositions of the basic operations. [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0025]] ^v37

## Terms

- **Knowledge model**: the meaning-bearing structure of a knowledge holding, formed by every filing decision [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0001]]
- **Knowledge document**: an atomic, structured knowledge unit distilled from raw material and optimised as a context artefact [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0007]]
- **Materialised context compression**: the condensation performed in advance that a model no longer has to reconstruct from the raw material [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0007]]
- **Knowledge transformation**: a systematic operation that converts information into a reusable, contextualised knowledge structure [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0011]]
- **Atomisation**: the division of a monolithic document into atomic units with the original becoming a map [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0013]]
- **Consolidation**: the merging of several documents into one with the redundancy removed [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0013]]
- **Self-containedness**: the requirement that the context a document needs stands inside the document rather than only in its links [[10_markdown/documents/vault-as-materialised-knowledge-model#^p0014]]

## Open questions

- The document states that the quality of the knowledge model bounds the quality of the output without naming a measure by which either quality could be assessed.
- The granularity conflict between a model built for human reading and one built for machine context is named as unresolved, and the document offers no criterion for deciding it per document type.
- The five-part systematics is declared an own coinage derived over two axes, and the document does not say which observation would show the axes to be the wrong ones.
- The transfer of an organisational knowledge-creation cycle onto operations on single documents is marked as a transfer, and the document leaves open what the transfer preserves and what it drops.
- The document describes a knowledge holding without a source-bound anchor chain, so it says nothing about how a statement inside a knowledge document is tied back to the passage that supports it.

## Appraisal

The vault document is the most theoretically ambitious of the four feeding artefacts and the only one that treats a knowledge holding as an object of modelling rather than as an aid to work. Two things make it valuable here. It supplies the properties and the acceptance test of a single knowledge document at a level of precision the lecture notes do not reach, and it declares its own epistemic status carefully, marking the five-part systematics as a coinage, the derivation over two axes as an inference and the mapping onto an organisational model as a transfer. That discipline is what lets a book chapter cite it without inheriting a claim to established terminology.

Its limits follow from its genre. It is a working text of one practice, its anchoring in documentation science rests on a web-supported check rather than on the reading of the cited works, and the correspondences it draws between its operations and older traditions are proposed rather than demonstrated. It also describes governance and provenance as principles without an enforcement mechanism, so the governed and source-bound conditions the manuscript needs for the Grounded Vault are stated here as demands and not as an architecture. Chapter 7 therefore uses it for the model reading and the document unit, and marks the anchor chain as this vault's own extension.
