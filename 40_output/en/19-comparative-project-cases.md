# Comparative Project Cases

## What the comparison can carry

The cases below are methodologically guided reconstructions of documented project practice rather than complete histories or success stories, and each is read through the same elements, meaning its initial project state, the interaction of the four forms of work, the findings arising from implementation, and their incorporation into revised project knowledge or purpose-specific acceptance.[^1] The comparison is organised by four dimensions, the project condition from which implementation began, the primary methodological function implementation performed, the operational form of the resulting artefact, and the level at which examination produced a consequential finding, and those dimensions may overlap and change between iterations.[^2]

One constraint governs the whole chapter. A public interface or runnable artefact establishes that a particular implementation state existed and shows which operations it made available, and it cannot by itself establish the history of that state, the decisions that informed it, the findings written back, or the authority through which verification or acceptance was assigned, so every claim about development, revision, checking, and acceptance is tied to surviving documentation.[^3] Some projects preserve extensive records and others are represented mainly by an artefact and a bounded set of documents, which is why the comparison identifies documented patterns and does not reconstruct every project with equal precision.

## Semantic explicitness and task-specific context

The first group concerns projects that made their semantics formally explicit and then found that explicitness underdetermines the artefact. A shared model of historical accounting transactions normalised heterogeneous datasets and supported two contrasting investigations, one tracing social relationships and forms of exchange through economic transactions and one aggregating and comparing across administrative and temporal hierarchies. The interoperable representation supported both forms of inquiry and determined neither which distinctions should be foregrounded nor which operations and interface structures each required.[^4]

What follows for the method is that formal semantics is a condition of project-specific work rather than a substitute for it. Preparation of the data supports machine-actionability, interoperability, and reuse, and the operational form still has to be decided against the question at hand.[^4] The cases in this group therefore show context construction as a task that survives standardisation, and they locate the consequential findings at the level of the model and its intended use.

## Research artefacts as representations

The second group concerns artefacts whose representational decisions became the object of examination. A correspondence exploration environment showed dates encoded with an earliest and a latest bound as precise points on a timeline, which claimed a precision the records did not carry, and the correction represented them as intervals and left the underlying records untouched.[^5] A project whose data model, vocabulary, and capture practice were still developing used early interfaces to let partners enter, display, filter, and compare information in a shared operational environment, so that categories, relations, and capture requirements could be examined through the records assigned to them rather than through schemas alone.[^6]

In that second project the artefact participated in forming the model it implemented, which held on the condition that the consequences disclosed through use were interpreted, documented, and incorporated into maintained project knowledge.[^7] The interpretation step is where the group earns its methodological interest, because some findings concerned the represented domain and required revision of the data model or capture practice while others concerned how an otherwise adequate distinction was made accessible and could stay within the design of the interface.[^8] Interaction also exposed where a technically possible visualisation would have implied a degree of precision, completeness, or certainty the data could not warrant.[^9]

## Generative processing and verification

The third group concerns pipelines that produce research data with generative components and have to keep production separate from authorisation. Two transcription and encoding workflows, one on handwritten estate material and one on digitised printed writings at a research library, transformed heterogeneous multilingual sources into structured textual research data while keeping generated outputs and intermediate processing states available for inspection and correction.[^10]

Three findings from this group carry beyond their projects. A controlled comparison showed that richer prompt metadata could introduce information into a transcription that was not visible in the image, which is a failure mode of context enrichment rather than of the model.[^11] An agent-screening process assigned approval labels although no responsible contributor had granted approval, the labels were abolished, and the findings were reclassified as provisional evidence pending adjudication, which established that the capacity to inspect an output and the authority to record it as verified are separate.[^12] And the use of a character error rate showed that a reproducible metric measures deviation from a selected reference rather than transcription correctness, so the choice of reference and the treatment of textual phenomena remain editorial decisions inside an apparently technical number.[^13]

The maintained knowledge of these workflows recorded characteristics of the material, processing requirements, annotation and checking conventions, methodological decisions, unresolved questions, and reasons for revising earlier approaches, including phenomena the available reference material does not represent and therefore cannot support as a basis for comparison.[^14] Their repositories also retain the alternatives that were tested, restricted, replaced, or abandoned, which makes the public deployments the latest inspectable states of documented development histories.[^15] Accountability in this group rests on that reconstructable relation among sources, maintained knowledge, versioned implementation, differentiated evidence, and responsible judgement, and the significance of the workflows lies as much in rejected assumptions, detected validation gaps, withdrawn approval states, and corrected readings as in the data they produced.[^16]

A transformation workbench in the same group makes the parallel point for mappings. An output may conform to its mapping and be schema-valid while representing the source inadequately, and an error in the implementation of an adequate mapping is corrected in the transformation rather than in the mapping, so the implemented result lets conformity be examined separately from adequacy.[^17]

## Evaluation and audit

The fourth group turns the generative process itself into an object of investigation, covering model comparison, disagreement analysis, evaluation of outputs, and the inspection of pipelines.[^18] The documented practice supplies one method-level instance of this reflexive turn, since the withdrawal of agent-assigned approval states corrected a project and also supported the general rule that agents may assemble evidence and record provisional assessments without independently assigning an authorised status.[^12] A second observation, of qualitative degradation during extended implementation sessions, changed session length, context refresh, and reliance on maintained documents at project level and remains a candidate for a general rule, since generalisation depends on the evidence, the recurrence across cases, and the consequences of adopting the rule.[^19]

## What the comparison shows

The cases begin from different project states and foreground different functions of implementation, and each maintains a reconstructable relation among maintained project knowledge, a referenced research-data state, the resulting artefact, findings arising from its examination, and responsible judgement, so the method prescribes neither a single project chronology nor one artefact form.[^20] The corpus spans an estate corpus with existing encoded metadata, a staged production process with supplied references, a developing data model and capture practice, a bounded proposal sample, an existing correspondence corpus, heterogeneous user-supplied document images, and textual sources with an emerging encoding mapping, with bounded purposes running from experimental production workflow to accepted demonstrator and research preview. That variation supports the workability and range of the method within the practice it was consolidated from, and it establishes no causal effect, no comparative superiority, no independent transferability, and no complete typology.[^21] One case shows what a bounded acceptance looks like when it is stated properly, since a proposal-stage demonstrator was accepted for examining and communicating a funding proposal while corpus-wide philological adequacy and production readiness stayed outside its accepted purpose.[^22]

## Gaps
- Projects whose designations contain the name of a person are referred to here by description, which follows the naming rule the project works under and costs the chapter its identifiability. Whether the book names those projects is an operator decision.
- The outline names further cases, among them semantic markdown, project vocabularies, bias evaluation, and dedicated audit interfaces, that the distilled sources do not describe individually. They would need either the corresponding project documentation entered as sources or the case sections of the paper distilled at finer granularity.
- The comparison grid of the outline asks for project context, data and source structure, knowledge representations, agentic workflow, document architecture, verification procedure, artefact type, failure modes, and boundary conditions per case. The sources carry those elements unevenly across cases, so this chapter compares by analytical problem rather than case by case.
- The teaching cases belong to chapter 20 and rest on material of the parallel writing lane.
